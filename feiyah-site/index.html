We are ending FGM/C, child marriage, and child beading in Marsabit County.

Feiyah Action Network works inside Rendille and Samburu communities, village by village. We sit with elders, mothers, fathers, and morans until protecting girls becomes the community's own decision, not an outsider's demand.

Three practices cut girls' childhoods short here.

FGM/C

Female genital mutilation and cutting remains widespread in pastoralist communities in Marsabit, despite being illegal in Kenya since 2011. It marks a girl as ready for marriage, often years before adulthood.

Child marriage

Girls are married off young, frequently right after being cut. Marriage ends their schooling and their childhood at the same time.

Child beading

In beading, a young man places beads on a girl, claiming her for a relationship the community never recognizes as marriage. Girls are left with pregnancies the community rejects, and with lasting harm.

Change that starts inside the manyatta, not outside it.

Laws alone have not protected girls in Marsabit. Communities protect girls when they decide to. Our work makes that decision possible.

01

Community dialogue

We hold sustained dialogues with elders, mothers, fathers, and morans across Rendille and Samburu villages. The goal is public, collective commitment to abandon FGM/C, child marriage, and beading, so no single family carries the social cost of refusing alone.

02

Women's economic power

A mother with her own income can say no to a bride price. We support women through handicraft enterprises, community banking groups, and livestock ownership, building the independence that makes refusal affordable.

03

Advocacy beyond the village

We are a founding member of the PARAN alliance of pastoralist organizations, and we work alongside ActionAid Kenya and Samburu Women Trust to push county and national institutions to enforce the laws that already protect girls.

Our founder, Rose Ngoilelo Orguba, worked in Marsabit for seven years and watched women and girls carry the heaviest burdens. She started this network so they would not carry them alone, and she leads by example: her own daughters will never be cut.

Rose comes from the Rendille community and was recognized by Kenya's Human Rights Defenders Coalition in 2018.
